Chapter 215: The Nobel Executioner

发布时间：2026/7/6 00:00:36

第二百一十五章：诺奖处刑人

Miyetsuko stood at the international podium, her face composed in a mask of virtuous indignation. "The regime is totalitarian," she declared to the cameras, pointing a slender finger at the maps of the neighboring nation. It was a bold claim, and predictably, the issue was highly divisive. The international community scrambled to define the situation, scratching their heads because everyone knew the neighboring state was, in fact, a functioning democracy. Yet, Miyetsuko held all the cards—and the microphone. She adjusted the heavy gold medal on her lapel, a glittering reminder that she is known for her advocacy of human rights. After all, she had won the Nobel Peace Prize for it.

美悦子站在国际讲台上，脸上挂着一副正义凛然的面具。“那个政权是极权主义，”她指着邻国地图向摄像机宣布。这是一个大胆的指控，不出所料，这个问题引起了极大的分歧。国际社会乱作一团，专家们挠着头不知所措，因为大家都知道那个邻国实际上是个运作良好的民主国家。然而，美悦子掌控着所有筹码——以及话语权。她整理了一下翻领上沉重的金质奖章，那闪闪发光的提醒着世人：她是以人权倡导者而闻名的。毕竟，她可是凭此获得了诺贝尔和平奖。

Not satisfied with just redefining democracy, she used the same speech to announce a new domestic policy. "From today, vandalism is a crime," she announced, her voice sweet as honey, "and those found guilty will be hanged."

她不仅重新定义了民主，还顺便宣布了一项新的国内政策。“从今天起，破坏公物即是犯罪，”她声音甜美如蜜，“凡罪名成立者，一律绞死。”

Back at the manor, the family was enjoying a quiet evening, treating the broadcast like a piece of low-brow entertainment.

庄园里，一家人正享受着宁静的傍晚，把这场电视直播当成低俗娱乐节目来看。

"Hanging people for graffiti?" Sayuri giggled, swirling her cocktail. "I wonder if that counts as 'human rights advocacy.' Do you think the Nobel committee gets a special invitation to the executions, or do they just send a 'thank you' card?"

“因为涂鸦就绞死人？”小百合咯咯笑着，晃动着手里的鸡尾酒，“不知道这算不算‘人权倡导’的一部分。你觉得诺贝尔委员会会收到绞刑现场的特别邀请函吗？还是他们只会收到一张‘感谢卡’？”

Rin laughed, leaning back in her chair. "She called a democracy 'totalitarian' while threatening to hang citizens for painting on walls. The irony is so thick you could cut it with a knife. And yet, the world keeps clapping. It’s absolutely magnificent."

铃大笑着，靠在椅子背上。“她管一个民主国家叫‘极权主义’，同时威胁要因为涂鸦把公民绞死。这讽刺意味浓得连刀都切不开。然而，世界还在为她鼓掌。这真是太壮观了。”

Fujiwara, who was busy trimming the hedges in the garden—carefully avoiding any vandalism—walked in, wiping sweat from his forehead. "I heard the news. Should I be worried about the stray weeds in the garden? Does pulling them out count as vandalism, or am I safe?"

藤原正在花园里忙着修剪树篱——小心翼翼地避免任何“破坏公物”的行为——他走进来，擦了擦额头的汗。“我听到新闻了。我需要担心花园里的杂草吗？拔掉它们算不算‘破坏公物’？还是说我很安全？”

Miyetsuko entered the room, looking utterly refreshed from her day of manufacturing reality. She sat down, accepted a drink from Fujiwara, and smirked. "Weeds are only 'vandalism' if they are beautiful. If they are ugly, they are 'public nuisances' and you are doing the state a service by removing them." She turned to the group, her eyes glinting with amusement. "And as for the Nobel committee? They’re terrified that if they revoke the prize, I’ll declare their headquarters a 'vandalized' zone and start the gallows construction."

美悦子走进房间，结束了一天制造现实的工作，看起来精神焕发。她坐下来，接过藤原递上的酒，冷笑一声。“杂草只有在美丽时才算‘破坏公物’。如果它们很丑，那就是‘公共祸害’，你清理它们是在为国效力。”她转向众人，眼中闪烁着戏谑的精光。“至于诺贝尔委员会？他们怕极了。他们怕一旦撤销我的奖项，我就会宣布他们的总部是‘破坏公物区’，然后开始在那儿建绞刑架。”
