Chapter 206: Weeds, Laws, and Venoms

发布时间：2026/7/5 21:05:20

第二百零六章：杂草、法令与毒液

Fujiwara, perhaps feeling a bit too confident in his "revitalized" state, decided to take on the estate’s maintenance himself. He bought a crate of industrial-strength herbicide, determined to clear the grounds. The garden was filled with daffodils, their bright, cheerful yellow heads mocking the chaos he was trying to suppress. However, there is a lack of consistency in his work; one moment he was spraying with surgical precision, the next he was wandering off to stare at the clouds. It was during one of these distracted moments, deep in the overgrowth, that he reached into a thicket and felt a sharp, burning prick. A local viper had claimed its territory. The family physician, looking grave, examined the swelling wound and delivered the news with a grim shake of his head: "There is no known antidote for this poison."

藤原或许是对自己“重获新生”后的状态过于自信，决定亲自接手庄园的维护工作。他买了一箱工业级除草剂，决心把院子清理干净。花园里种满了水仙花，那明亮而欢快的黄色花朵，仿佛在嘲笑他试图压制的混乱。然而，他在工作上缺乏一贯性；上一秒他还在进行外科手术般的精准喷洒，下一秒就走神去盯着云彩发呆。就在他深陷杂草丛中的某次走神时，他伸手探入灌木丛，感觉到一阵灼热的刺痛。一条当地毒蛇守护着自己的领地。家庭医生面色凝重地检查了肿胀的伤口，摇着头给出了诊断：“这种毒素目前没有已知的解毒剂。”

Inside the study, Miyetsuko was in a legislative frenzy. The government legislates on various issues, and she had passed over eighty laws in a single week, governing everything from the height of garden fences to the specific shade of paint for residential doors. The populace was starting to chafe. Complaints were mounting that some groups are marginalized in society, pushed to the fringes by these endless, suffocating regulations. The middle class was particularly vocal; his lifestyle was typical of the bourgeois, yet they found themselves trapped in a cycle of stagnation. They complained about the suffocating feeling of living in a bungalow, unable to move up in a society where every upward step was now heavily taxed and strictly codified.

书房里，美悦子正陷入立法狂热。政府在各种议题上立法，她在短短一周内就通过了八十多项法律，大到庭院围栏的高度，小到住宅大门的漆面颜色，事无巨细。民众开始感到厌烦。越来越多的抱怨声称，社会中有一些群体被边缘化了，在这些无休止且令人窒息的规章制度下，他们被推向了社会的边缘。中产阶级尤为不满；那种典型的资产阶级生活方式已然固化，他们发现自己陷入了停滞的循环。他们抱怨住在平房里那种令人窒息的感觉，在这个每一级上升空间都被重税和严格条文锁死的社会里，他们再也爬不动了。

Rin sat in the lounge, lazily watching a televised endurance event. The race was gruelling, with competitors collapsing in the mud as the cameras zoomed in on their sweat-drenched, agonizing faces. She sipped her tea, unfazed by the suffering on screen. "Look at them," she murmured to the empty room. "Running themselves into the ground for a ribbon. It’s almost as pathetic as the taxpayers trying to navigate Miyetsuko’s new 'Bungalow Efficiency Act'."

铃坐在休息室里，懒洋洋地看着电视里的耐力赛。这场比赛极其艰苦，随着摄像机镜头拉近，选手们满脸汗水、痛苦不堪，纷纷倒在泥地里。她抿了一口茶，对屏幕上的苦难无动于衷。“瞧瞧他们，”她对着空荡的屋子喃喃自语，“为了条丝带把自己跑得半死不活。这和那些试图钻研美悦子‘平房效率法案’的纳税人一样，简直可悲。”

Back in the bedroom, Fujiwara lay shivering, the poison coursing through his veins, but he was grinning. The drama, the legislative chaos, and his own brush with death—it was the most excitement he’d had in years. "If I survive this," he rasped to Sayuri, who was applying a cold compress to his forehead with a giggle, "you have to promise to burn the daffodils. They’re the ones that hid the snake."

卧室里，藤原躺在床上瑟瑟发抖，毒液在血管中流窜，但他却在傻笑。这种戏剧性、立法带来的混乱，以及他自己与死神的擦肩而过——这是他多年来经历过最刺激的事。“如果我能活下来，”他沙哑着声音对小百合说，小百合正咯咯笑着往他额头上贴冷敷袋，“你得答应我把那些水仙花全烧了。就是它们把蛇给藏起来的。”
