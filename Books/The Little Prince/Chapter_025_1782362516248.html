CHAPTER XXV

发布时间：2026/8/8 19:55:55

第二十五章

"Men," said the little prince, "set out on their way in express trains, but they do not know what they are looking for. Then they rush about, and get excited, and turn round and round ..."

“那些人，”小王子说，“他们坐上特快列车出发，却不知道自己要找的是什么。于是他们忙得团团转，焦躁不安，绕了一圈又一圈……”

And he added:

接着他又说：

"It is not worth the trouble ..."

“这真不值得费那个劲……”

The well that we had come to was not like the wells of the Sahara. The wells of the Sahara are mere holes dug in the sand. This one was like a well in a village. But there was no village here, and I thought I must be dreaming ...

我们到达的那口井，和撒哈拉的井不一样。撒哈拉的井只是在沙地上挖的洞。这口井却像村庄里的井。可这里并没有村庄，我以为自己一定是在做梦……

"It is strange," I said to the little prince. "Everything is ready for use: the pulley, the bucket, the rope ..."

“真奇怪，”我对小王子说。“一切都现成好用：辘轳、水桶、绳子……”

He laughed, touched the rope, and set the pulley to working. And the pulley moaned, like an old weathervane which the wind has long since forgotten.

他笑了，碰了碰绳子，转动起辘轳来。辘轳发出一声呻吟，像是一个被风遗忘了很久的旧风标。

"Do you hear?" said the little prince. "We have wakened the well, and it is singing ..." I did not want him to tire himself with the rope. "Leave it to me," I said. "It is too heavy for you."

“你听见了吗？”小王子说。“我们唤醒了这口井，它在唱歌呢……”我不想让他被绳子累着。“让我来吧，”我说，“这对你来说太重了。”

I hoisted the bucket slowly to the edge of the well and set it there—happy, tired as I was, over my achievement. The song of the pulley was still in my ears, and I could see the sunlight shimmer in the still trembling water.

我慢慢地把水桶摇到井沿，把它放好——虽然很累，可我还是为自己的成就而高兴。辘轳的歌声还在我耳边回响，我能看见阳光在微微颤动的水面上闪烁。

"I am thirsty for this water," said the little prince. "Give me some of it to drink ..." And I understood what he had been looking for.

“我渴了，想喝这水，”小王子说。“给我喝一点吧……”这下我明白他一直在寻找什么了。

I raised the bucket to his lips. He drank, his eyes closed. It was as sweet as some special festival treat. This water was indeed a different thing from ordinary nourishment. Its sweetness was born of the walk under the stars, the song of the pulley, the effort of my arms. It was good for the heart, like a present. When I was a little boy, the lights of the Christmas tree, the music of the Midnight Mass, the tenderness of smiling faces, used to make up, so, the radiance of the gifts I received.

我把水桶举到他唇边。他喝着，双眼紧闭。那水像节日里某种特别的款待一样甘甜。这水确实不同于普通的解渴之物。它的甘甜，源自星光下的跋涉，源自辘轳的歌声，源自我双臂的辛劳。它对心是有好处的，就像一件礼物。在我还是个小男孩的时候，圣诞树上的灯光、子夜弥撒的音乐、一张张笑脸的温情，就是这样为收到的礼物增添了光彩。

"The men where you live," said the little prince, "raise five thousand roses in the same garden—and they do not find in it what they are looking for."

“你住的地方的那些人，”小王子说，“在同一个花园里种了五千朵玫瑰——可他们却找不到自己要找的东西。”

"They do not find it," I replied.

“他们找不到，”我回答说。

"And yet what they are looking for could be found in one single rose, or in a little water."

“然而他们要找的东西，其实可以从一朵玫瑰、或者一点点水里找到。”

"Yes, that is true," I said. And the little prince added:

“是的，没错，”我说。小王子又加了一句：

"But the eyes are blind. One must look with the heart..."

“可是眼睛是盲目的。必须用心去看……”

I had drunk the water. I breathed easily. At sunrise the sand is the color of honey. And that honey color was making me happy, too. What brought me, then, this sense of grief?

我喝过水，呼吸顺畅了。日出时分，沙地是蜂蜜色的。这蜂蜜的颜色也让我快乐。然而，是什么让我感到了这份忧伤呢？

"You must keep your promise," said the little prince, softly, as he sat down beside me once more. "What promise?"

“你得遵守你的诺言，”小王子又在我身边坐下来，轻轻地说。“什么诺言？”

"You know—a muzzle for my sheep ... I am responsible for this flower ..."

“你知道的——给我的绵羊画一个嘴套……我要对这朵花负责……”

I took my rough drafts of drawings out of my pocket. The little prince looked them over, and laughed as he said:

我从口袋里掏出我那些画稿。小王子看了看，笑着说：

"Your baobabs—they look a little like cabbages." "Oh!" I had been so proud of my baobabs!

“你的猴面包树——看起来有点像卷心菜。”“哦！”我还一直为我的猴面包树感到那么自豪呢！

"Your fox—his ears look a little like horns; and they are too long." And he laughed again.

“你的狐狸——它的耳朵有点像角；而且还太长了。”他又笑了。

"You are not fair, little prince," I said. "I don't know how to draw anything except boa constrictors from the outside and boa constrictors from the inside."

“你不公平，小王子，”我说，“除了肚皮敞开和肚皮合上的大蟒蛇，我什么都不会画。”

"Oh, that will be all right," he said, "children understand."

“哦，没关系的，”他说，“孩子们看得懂。”

So then I made a pencil sketch of a muzzle. And as I gave it to him my heart was torn. "You have plans that I do not know about," I said. But he did not answer me. He said to me, instead:

于是我用铅笔画了一个嘴套。把它交给他的时候，我心里一阵绞痛。“你有些计划是我不知道的，”我说。可他没有回答我，而是对我说：

"You know—my descent to the earth ... Tomorrow will be its anniversary." Then, after a silence, he went on: "I came down very near here." And he flushed.

“你知道——我降落到地球上……明天就是一周年了。”沉默了一会儿，他又说：“我降落在离这儿很近的地方。”他脸红了。

And once again, without understanding why, I had a queer sense of sorrow. One question, however, occurred to me:

我又一次，不知为什么，感到一阵莫名的悲伤。不过，我想起了一个问题：

"Then it was not by chance that on the morning when I first met you—a week ago—you were strolling along like that, all alone, a thousand miles from any inhabited region? You were on your way back to the place where you landed?"

“那么，一周前我第一次遇见你的那天早上，你那样独自一人在远离人烟上千英里的地方散步，就不是偶然了？你是在返回你降落的地方？”

The little prince flushed again. And I added, with some hesitancy:

小王子的脸又红了。我有些犹豫地补充道：

"Perhaps it was because of the anniversary?"

“也许是因为周年纪念？”

The little prince flushed once more. He never answered questions—but when one flushes does that not mean "Yes"?

小王子的脸更红了。他从不回答问题——可是，一个人脸红，不就等于在说“是的”吗？

"Ah," I said to him, "I am a little frightened—" But he interrupted me.

“啊，”我对他说，“我有点怕——”但他打断了我。

"Now you must work. You must return to your engine. I will be waiting for you here. Come back tomorrow evening ..."

“现在你得干活了。你得回到你的发动机那儿去。我在这儿等你。明天傍晚再来……”

But I was not reassured. I remembered the fox. One runs the risk of weeping a little, if one lets himself be tamed ...

可我还是放不下心。我想起了那只狐狸。如果让自己被驯化了，人总是要冒一点流泪的风险的……
