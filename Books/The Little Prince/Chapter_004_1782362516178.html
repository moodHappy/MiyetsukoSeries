第四章

I had thus learned a second fact of great importance: this was that the planet the little prince came from was scarcely any larger than a house!

就这样，我又了解到了第二个非常重要的事实：小王子来自的那个星球几乎不比一栋房子大多少！

But that did not really surprise me much. I knew very well that in addition to the great planets—such as the Earth, Jupiter, Mars, Venus—to which we have given names, there are also hundreds of others, some of which are so small that one has a hard time seeing them through the telescope. When an astronomer discovers one of these he does not give it a name, but only a number. He might call it, for example, ¨Asteroid 325.¨

但这并没有让我感到太惊讶。我清楚地知道，除了那些已经被我们命名的大行星——比如地球、木星、火星、金星——之外，还有成百上千颗其他的星球，其中有些太小了，即使用望远镜也很难看到。当一位天文学家发现其中一颗时，他不会给它取名字，而只是给它一个编号。例如，他可能会叫它“325号小行星”。

I have serious reason to believe that the planet from which the little prince came is the asteroid known as B-612.

我有充分的理由相信，小王子来自的那个星球就是被称为B-612号的小行星。

This asteroid has only once been seen through the telescope. That was by a Turkish astronomer, in

这颗小行星只在望远镜里被看到过一次。那是由一位土耳其天文学家发现的，在

* 1909年。

On making his discovery, the astronomer had presented it to the International Astronomical Congress, in a great demonstration. But he was in Turkish costume, and so nobody would believe what he said.

做出这个发现后，这位天文学家在国际天文学大会上做了一次盛大的论证报告。但他当时穿着土耳其传统服装，所以没有人相信他说的话。

Grown-ups are like that...

大人们就是这样……

Fortunately, however, for the reputation of Asteroid B-612, a Turkish dictator made a law that his subjects, under pain of death, should change to European costume. So in 1920 the astronomer gave his demonstration all over again, dressed with impressive style and elegance. And this time everybody accepted his report.

不过，幸运的是，为了B-612号小行星的声誉，一位土耳其独裁者下令，要求他的国民改穿欧洲服装，违者处以死刑。因此，在1920年，这位天文学家穿着令人印象深刻的优雅西装，又重新做了一次报告。而这一次，所有人都接受了他的报告。

If I have told you these details about the asteroid, and made a note of its number for you, it is on account of the grown-ups and their ways. When you tell them that you have made a new friend, they never ask you any questions about essential matters. They never say to you, ¨What does his voice sound like? What games does he love best? Does he collect butterflies?¨ Instead, they demand: ¨How old is he? How many brothers has he? How much does he weigh? How much money does his father make?¨ Only from these figures do they think they have learned anything about him.

如果我向你们讲述了关于这颗小行星的这些细节，并且为你们记下了它的编号，那都是因为大人们以及他们行事方式的缘故。当你告诉他们你交了一个新朋友时，他们从来不会问你任何关于本质问题的事情。他们绝不会对你说：“他的声音听起来是什么样的？他最喜欢玩什么游戏？他收集蝴蝶标本吗？”相反，他们会盘问：“他多大了？他有几个兄弟？他有多重？他父亲挣多少钱？”只有通过这些数字，他们才认为自己了解了他。

If you were to say to the grown-ups: ¨I saw a beautiful house made of rosy brick, with geraniums in the windows and doves on the roof,¨ they would not be able to get any idea of that house at all. You would have to say to them: ¨I saw a house that cost $20,000.¨ Then they would exclaim: ¨Oh, what a pretty house that is!¨

如果你对大人们说：“我看到了一栋用玫瑰色砖块建成的漂亮房子，窗户上开着天竺葵，屋顶上停着鸽子，”他们根本无法想象出那栋房子是什么样子。你必须对他们说：“我看到了一栋价值两万美元的房子。”这时他们就会惊呼：“哦，那房子真漂亮！”

Just so, you might say to them: ¨The proof that the little prince existed is that he was charming, that he laughed, and that he was looking for a sheep. If anybody wants a sheep, that is a proof that he exists.¨ And what good would it do to tell them that? They would shrug their shoulders, and treat you like a child. But if you said to them: ¨The planet he came from is Asteroid B-612,¨ then they would be convinced, and leave you in peace from their questions.

同样地，你可能会对他们说：“小王子存在的证明就是他很迷人，他会笑，而且他在找一只羊。如果一个人想要一只羊，那就是他存在的证明。”可是跟他们说这些有什么用呢？他们只会耸耸肩，把你当成小孩子对待。但如果你对他们说：“他来自的那个星球是B-612号小行星”，他们就会被说服，不再用各种问题来烦你了。

They are like that. One must not hold it against them. Children should always show great forbearance toward grown-up people.

他们就是这样。人们不应该对此苛责他们。孩子们对大人们总是应该表现出极大的宽容。

But certainly, for us who understand life, figures are a matter of indifference. I should have liked to begin this story in the fashion of the fairy-tales. I should have like to say: ¨Once upon a time there was a little prince who lived on a planet that was scarcely any bigger than himself, and who had need of a sheep ...¨

但是毫无疑问，对于我们这些真正懂得生活的人来说，数字根本无关紧要。我本来很想用童话故事的方式来开始这个故事。我本来很想说：“从前，有一个小王子，他住在一个几乎不比他自己大多少的星球上，他需要一只羊……”

To those who understand life, that would have given a much greater air of truth to my story.

对于那些懂得生活的人来说，这样会让我的故事听起来真实得多。

For I do not want any one to read my book carelessly. I have suffered too much grief in setting down these memories. Six years have already passed since my friend went away from me, with his sheep. If I try to describe him here, it is to make sure that I shall not forget him. To forget a friend is sad. Not every one has had a friend. And if I forget him, I may become like the grown-ups who are no longer interested in anything but figures ...

因为我不希望任何人草率地读我的书。在写下这些记忆时，我承受了太多的悲伤。我的朋友带着他的羊离开我，已经过去六年了。如果我在这里试着描绘他，那是为了确保我不会忘记他。忘记一个朋友是悲哀的。并不是每个人都有过朋友。如果我忘记了他，我可能会变得像那些除了数字对其他任何事情都不再感兴趣的大人们一样……

It is for that purpose, again, that I have bought a box of paints and some pencils. It is hard to take up drawing again at my age, when I have never made any pictures except those of the boa constrictor from the outside and the boa constrictor from the inside, since I was six. I shall certainly try to make my portraits as true to life as possible. But I am not at all sure of success. One drawing goes along all right,

也正是为了这个目的，我又买了一盒颜料和一些铅笔。在我的这个年纪重新开始画画是很难的，毕竟自从我六岁以来，除了画过从外面看和从里面看的蟒蛇之外，我就再也没有画过任何画。我一定会尽量把画像画得栩栩如生。但我完全不敢保证能成功。有一张画得还挺像，

and another has no resemblance to its subject. I make some errors, too, in the little prince's height: in one place he is too tall and in another too short. And I feel some doubts about the color of his costume. So I fumble along as best I can, now good, now bad, and I hope generally fair-to-middling.

而另一张却和本人毫无相似之处。在画小王子的身高时，我也犯了一些错误：这个地方他太高了，那个地方他又太矮了。对于他衣服的颜色我也有些拿不准。所以，我只好尽我所能地摸索着画，画得时好时坏，我只希望总体上还说得过去。

In certain more important details I shall make mistakes, also. But that is something that will not be my fault. My friend never explained anything to me. He thought, perhaps, that I was like himself. But I, alas, do not know how to see sheep through the walls of boxes. Perhaps I am a little like the grown-ups. I have had to grow old.

在某些更重要的细节上，我也会犯错。但这并不是我的错。我的朋友从来不向我解释任何事情。他或许以为我和他是一样的人。可是，唉，我并不知道该怎么透过箱子的墙壁看到里面的羊。也许我有那么一点像大人们了。我不得不渐渐老去。
