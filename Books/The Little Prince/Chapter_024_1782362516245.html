CHAPTER XXIV

发布时间：2026/8/8 19:50:01

第二十四章

It was now the eighth day since I had had my accident in the desert, and I had listened to the story of the merchant as I was drinking the last drop of my water supply.

这是我在沙漠里发生事故后的第八天，我一边听那个商人的故事，一边喝掉了自己储备的最后一滴水。

"Ah," I said to the little prince, "these memories of yours are very charming; but I have not yet succeeded in repairing my plane; I have nothing more to drink; and I, too, should be very happy if I could walk at my leisure toward a spring of fresh water!"

“啊，”我对小王子说，“你的这些回忆非常动人；可我的飞机还没有修好；我再也没有水可喝了；如果能悠悠闲闲地朝一汪清泉走去，我呀，也会非常高兴的！”

"My friend the fox—" the little prince said to me.

“我的狐狸朋友——”小王子对我说。

"My dear little man, this is no longer a matter that has anything to do with the fox!" "Why not?" "Because I am about to die of thirst..."

“我亲爱的小家伙，这事已经跟狐狸没有关系了！”“为什么没有关系？”“因为我就要渴死了……”

He did not follow my reasoning, and he answered me:

他没有理会我的道理，回答我说：

"It is a good thing to have had a friend, even if one is about to die. I, for instance, am very glad to have had a fox as a friend ..."

“曾经有过一个朋友是件好事，哪怕人快要死了也是一样。比如说我，我就很高兴曾经有过一只狐狸当朋友……”

"He has no way of guessing the danger," I said to myself. "He has never been either hungry or thirsty. A little sunshine is all he needs ..."

“他根本猜不到有多危险，”我心想。“他从来没挨过饿，也没受过渴。一点点阳光就足够他需要的了……”

But he looked at me steadily, and replied to my thought:

可他却稳稳地望着我，回应了我心中所想：

"I am thirsty, too. Let us look for a well..."

“我也渴了。我们去找一口井吧……”

I made a gesture of weariness. It is absurd to look for a well, at random, in the immensity of the desert. But nevertheless we started walking.

我做了个疲惫的手势。在茫茫沙漠里漫无目的地找一口井，这太荒唐了。可我们还是走了起来。

When we had trudged along for several hours, in silence, the darkness fell, and the stars began to come out. Thirst had made me a little feverish, and I looked at them as if I were in a dream. The little prince's last words came reeling back into my memory:

我们默默地跋涉了好几个小时之后，夜幕降临了，星星一颗颗地亮了起来。口渴让我有点发烧，我望着星星，恍如在梦中。小王子最后说的话在我记忆里打转：

"Then you are thirsty, too?" I demanded.

“那么你也渴了吗？”我问他。

But he did not reply to my question. He merely said to me:

可他没有回答我的问题，只是对我说：

"Water may also be good for the heart..."

“水对心也是有好处的……”

I did not understand this answer, but I said nothing. I knew very well that it was impossible to cross-examine him.

我不懂这句话的意思，但我什么也没说。我非常清楚，盘问他是不可能的。

He was tired. He sat down. I sat down beside him. And, after a little silence, he spoke again: "The stars are beautiful, because of a flower that cannot be seen."

他累了。他坐了下来。我在他身边坐下。沉默了一小会儿，他又开口说：“星星很美，因为有一朵看不见的花。”

I replied, "Yes, that is so." And, without saying anything more, I looked across the ridges of sand that were stretched out before us in the moonlight.

我回答说：“是的，是这样。”我没再多说什么，只是望着月光下在我们面前铺展开去的沙脊。

"The desert is beautiful," the little prince added.

“沙漠很美，”小王子补充道。

And that was true. I have always loved the desert. One sits down on a desert sand dune, sees nothing, hears nothing. Yet through the silence something throbs, and gleams ...

这倒是真的。我一直都喜欢沙漠。一个人坐在沙丘上，什么也看不见，什么也听不见。可是，在这静寂之中，却有什么东西在跳动，在闪光……

"What makes the desert beautiful," said the little prince, "is that somewhere it hides a well..."

“让沙漠变得美丽的，”小王子说，“是在它的某个地方藏着一眼井……”

I was astonished by a sudden understanding of that mysterious radiation of the sands. When I was a little boy I lived in an old house, and legend told us that a treasure was buried there. To be sure, no one had ever known how to find it; perhaps no one had ever even looked for it. But it cast an enchantment over that house. My home was hiding a secret in the depths of its heart...

我突然领悟了那沙地所散发出的神秘光芒意味着什么，这让我大为惊讶。我小的时候住在一座老房子里，传说那房子底下埋着一笔宝藏。当然，从来没人知道该怎么找到它；也许根本没人去找过。可这传说却给那座房子施了魔法。我的家，在它心灵深处，藏着一个秘密……

"Yes," I said to the little prince. "The house, the stars, the desert—what gives them their beauty is something that is invisible!"

“是的，”我对小王子说。“房子、星星、沙漠——赋予它们美丽的东西是眼睛看不见的！”

"I am glad," he said, "that you agree with my fox."

“我很高兴，”他说，“你和我那只狐狸的看法一样了。”

As the little prince dropped off to sleep, I took him in my arms and set out walking once more. I felt deeply moved, and stirred. It seemed to me that I was carrying a very fragile treasure. It seemed to me, even, that there was nothing more fragile on all Earth. In the moonlight I looked at his pale forehead, his closed eyes, his locks of hair that trembled in the wind, and I said to myself: "What I see here is nothing but a shell. What is most important is invisible ..."

小王子渐渐睡着了，我把他抱在怀里，又重新上路。我心里涌动着深深的感动。我觉得自己怀抱着一个极其脆弱的宝物。我甚至觉得，在这整个地球上，再没有什么比他更脆弱的了。借着月光，我望着他那苍白的额头、紧闭的双眼、在风中轻颤的发绺，我对自己说：“我所看到的，不过是一具躯壳罢了。最重要的东西是看不见的……”

As his lips opened slightly with the suspicion of a half-smile, I said to myself, again: "What moves me so deeply, about this little prince who is sleeping here, is his loyalty to a flower—the image of a rose that shines through his whole being like the flame of a lamp, even when he is asleep ..." And I felt him to be more fragile still. I felt the need of protecting him, as if he himself were a flame that might be extinguished by a little puff of wind ...

他的嘴唇微微张开，露出一丝隐约的微笑，我又对自己说：“这个熟睡的小王子之所以让我如此深深地感动，是他对一朵花的忠诚——那朵玫瑰的影子，像一盏灯的火焰，贯穿他的整个生命，哪怕在他睡着的时候，也在熠熠发光……”我感觉到他比先前更加脆弱了。我觉得需要保护他，仿佛他自己就是一团火焰，只需一阵微风，就可能熄灭……

And, as I walked on so, I found the well, at daybreak.

就这样走着走着，在拂晓时分，我找到了那口井。
